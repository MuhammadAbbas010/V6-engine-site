AHow this model moves

Nothing on this page is keyframed animation. A single scalar — crank angle, 0° to 720°, one complete four-stroke cycle — drives every moving component, and each is solved from that angle at draw time.

Piston position. Solved from the slider-crank relation. The distance along a bank axis is s = d + √(L² − r² − dx² + d²), where d is the projection of the crank pin onto that axis, r the 43 mm crank radius, L the 150 mm rod and dx the 21 mm bank offset. Because it is solved rather than animated, rod length holds constant to 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ mm at every angle and measured stroke lands on exactly 86.000 mm.

Connecting rods. Not rotated by a formula. Each rod is aimed by pointing it from its own gudgeon pin at its own crank pin, so it cannot separate from either end however the assembly is manipulated.

Valve lift. A raised-cosine lobe 240° wide in crank degrees, centred on each cylinder's own intake or exhaust event and offset by its position in the 1-4-2-5-3-6 firing order. Camshafts turn at half crank speed. The same ordering sequences the combustion events inside the bores.

The fuel model is deliberately simple and deliberately visible. Burn rate scales with rpm. Below 18% of a tank the mixture leans out, so delivered rpm falls short of the target and oscillates; at zero the engine stalls until refuelled.
